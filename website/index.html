01 — Voice

How a passage gets its voice

Every passage is written in a named authorial persona: a particular way of hedging, of conceding ground, and of deciding how late to commit to a claim. The persona is fixed before the writing starts, and it brings its own vocabulary of qualification with it.

Voice is the part students learn fastest, because it is the part that feels like a person — the hedge that always lands in the same place, the concession that always arrives one paragraph before the turn. So it is the part we vary hardest, and vary consistently. Being different is not left to chance: each voice carries its own hedging inventory and its own commitment curve, and no set ships until it has been measured for authorial distance from the ones already delivered.

Why it keeps happening

  • A human writer

    Even excellent writers have one voice and one instinct for where an argument should turn. Fifteen passages in, the fingerprints are everywhere — and no writer can prove passage fifteen is structurally unlike passage one.

    Leak: house style

  • A single AI prompt

    One prompt encodes one frozen essay shape: the same destabilised opening, the same corrective middle, the same artfully reopened ending. Every output is the same essay wearing a different topic.

    Leak: one skeleton, reprinted

  • A similarity checker

    Embedding checks compare meaning. Two passages on unrelated topics can share an identical argumentative skeleton and still score as complete strangers. Students notice structural repetition long before any topic overlap exists.

    Leak: structure is invisible to it

Nobody audits mock content for structural repetition. So we built the instrument first, and the engine around it.

03 — Method

How a passage gets its shape

The shape of the argument is decided before a word is written, and it is decided by a sampler, not by a writer with habits.

Seven choices are fixed in advance: how the argument moves, whose voice tells it, how the paragraphs are paced, when the thesis becomes visible, which misreadings the passage will invite, and how the six questions will interrogate it. Only after that is the prose written to fit. The writing step never gets to choose the shape — which is exactly the choice that leaks.

A family just used is locked for the next 25 sets, a question topology for 12, a voice for 10. The combination is then checked for distance from the last fifty sets before anything is written.

What happens to one set

  1. The shape is drawn and checked

    Seven components sampled under exclusion windows, filtered by compatibility rules, then distance-checked against the last fifty blueprints. Near-clones are rejected here, before anything is written.

  2. The passage is written to that shape

    Paragraph function, word range, cadence, when the thesis surfaces, how it ends, and a list of phrases it may not use. The writing step invents every sentence and chooses no structure.

  3. The structure is re-derived blind

    A separate pass reads the finished passage without the plan and works out its structure from scratch. If the turn landed in ¶3 instead of ¶4, the passage is rewritten with that specific correction — not discarded.

  4. Novelty is checked before questions exist

    Movement, pacing, conviction, voice and meaning are compared against your corpus. A passage that resembles its history dies here, cheaply, and a new shape is drawn.

  5. Six questions are built from the plan

    Generated from the blueprint’s question topology rather than reverse-engineered from the finished prose. Wrong options quote the exact misreadings the passage was built to invite.

  6. It is solved blind and judged

    An independent solver answers all six without seeing the key. A rubric scores the set against its own intent. Any disagreement goes to a person rather than to an automatic rewrite.

04 — The audit

Nine things we check before a set ships

Each channel below is a sentence we do not want your students to be able to say. They are listed in the students’ own words, because that is the form the problem takes in a classroom.

Every finished set is scored against the trailing hundred on all nine. Meaning is one of the nine. The other eight are what a similarity checker cannot see, and any single breach stops the set.

Said by a student

  • 01

    “These passages always argue the same way.”

    Argument skeleton. How much of the underlying shape this set shares with recent ones, weighted by what students notice first.

  • 02

    “¶2 raises the objection, ¶4 resolves it.”

    Paragraph choreography. The order of argumentative moves, tracked like a chess line. Repeated orders and repeated transitions are both caught.

  • 03

    “The author always commits late, so answers live at the end.”

    Conviction arc. How committed the author sounds, paragraph by paragraph. The most learnable signature a writer has, and the one we force to vary.

  • 04

    “The short paragraph is always the pivot.”

    Pacing. Paragraph lengths, sentence cadence, where the pivot sits — the music of the prose, blocked from repeating.

  • 05

    “Q1 is main-idea. The last one is tone.”

    Question mix. Type, order and difficulty curve across the six. There is no fixed question formula to learn because there is no fixed question formula.

  • 06

    “Cut the extreme option and the broad one. Done.”

    Wrong-answer logic. The mechanisms of wrongness are tracked as a distribution, so elimination habits that work on one set fail on the next.

  • 07

    “These all read like one writer.”

    Voice separation. Function-word forensics — the method used to attribute anonymous authorship — confirms different voices really are different writers.

  • 08

    “When unsure: longest option, most hedged, or C.”

    Answer-key behaviour. Letters, option lengths and hedging tested for exploitable trends across your whole series, not just inside one set.

  • 09

    “We have basically read this passage before.”

    Topic distance. The oldest check in the industry. Still enforced, now one voice among nine rather than the only one.

05 — Answer keys

The guessing shortcuts, closed

Some tells do not live in the prose at all. They live in the keys, and every serious student has been taught them. These four are closed by construction rather than by hoping the writing behaves.

Four heuristics

  • “Pick C when unsure.”

    Answer letters are decided as a plan before the questions exist, then assigned mechanically — the options come back unlabelled, so nothing can cluster the keys. Letter behaviour is then tested across your whole series, not one paper.

  • “The longest option is right.”

    Per set, the correct option may be strictly longest in at most two of six questions. The main-idea question gets its own budget across the corpus, because that is the one students check first.

  • “The most hedged option is right.”

    Each authorial voice carries its own hedging inventory, so qualification patterns differ from set to set — and one distractor profile exists specifically to make over-hedged options wrong.

  • “Extreme words mean wrong.”

    Lexical extremity is treated as a wasted distractor. Traps are built from the passage’s own logic — misreadings a strong reader could genuinely commit — so scanning for always and never eliminates nothing.

06 — Disputes

When your faculty disputes a key

This is the question that decides whether outsourced content is worth the trouble, so it is worth answering before the pitch.

Every set is answered by an independent solver that never sees the key. When it disagrees, that disagreement goes to a person — never quietly regenerated away — because a defensible dispute means either a broken key or a genuinely brutal question, and both deserve eyes. Where a rival reading is defensible, we rebuild the question inside 72 hours. Disputed items get replaced, not argued about.

What can happen to a set

  • Ships

    Approved

    Passed structure, novelty, blind solve, rubric and key-hygiene checks, then reviewed before release.

  • Held

    Needs review

    Complete but flagged — a low rubric score or a key-hygiene breach routes it to a person. It is never approved automatically.

  • Held

    Solver dispute

    The blind solver read it differently. A human adjudicates; defensible disputes get the question rebuilt.

  • Killed

    Rejected for novelty

    Too close to something in your history. Recomposed from scratch — and its fingerprint still joins the corpus, so nothing may resemble it either.

Sets that die are recorded with the reason. Being able to see what was rejected is what makes the approvals worth anything.

09 — Questions

Questions institutes ask

Before signing

Is this AI-generated content?

The passages are produced by an engine we built, and that is precisely why they can be audited. A human writer cannot prove their fifteenth passage is structurally unlike their first. We can, on nine measures, for every set, against your full history.

What no set ever is: raw generator output. A blind solve, a rubric judgement, key-hygiene budgets and human review all stand between generation and delivery. The blind audition exists because we are happy to be tested on the result rather than the method.

What happens when our faculty dispute an answer key?

Keys are independently blind-solved before delivery, and every rationale names the misreading each wrong option targets, so most disputes resolve on the rationale alone. Where a rival reading is genuinely defensible, we rebuild the question within 72 hours.

Who owns the content we receive?

You hold an exclusive licence to your delivered sets. No other client receives them, nor any set built on the same argumentative skeleton. We retain the structural system and the audit metadata, which is what makes that exclusivity enforceable rather than merely promised.

Which exams do you cover?

Calibrated for CAT-level VARC today, with passage lengths, question styles and difficulty tiers matched to the real paper. CLAT, OMET and GRE-style variants are available on request; the underlying libraries are exam-agnostic and only the surface parameters change.

How fast can a full mock series land?

Delivery is weekly against your mock calendar. A complete 10-mock VARC series lands within one month of calibration; week one locks lengths, difficulty curves and format to your paper pattern.

If every institute buys this, what is our edge?

No two clients ever share an argumentative skeleton — that is a constraint in the database, not a promise in a contract — and we serve one institute per category per city.

More fundamentally, what compounds is the memory of what your students have already seen. That history is yours, and nobody starting fresh can reproduce it.
