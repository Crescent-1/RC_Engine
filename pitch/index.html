04

Nine ways we stop pattern-matching

Aspirants learn your content’s habits. Each channel below tracks one habit. If any channel says a pattern is forming against your history, the set never ships.

01 Argument skeleton

“These passages always argue the same way.”

Every passage is built on one of 32 distinct argumentative skeletons; no two recent sets share one, and your corpus stays balanced across them.

02 Paragraph choreography

“Para 2 raises the objection; para 4 resolves it.”

The sequence of argumentative moves is tracked like a chess line—repeated move-orders are detected and rejected.

03 Conviction arc

“The author commits late—answers live in the late paragraphs.”

How strongly the author appears committed to the thesis, paragraph by paragraph, is forced to vary. This rhythm is what students most often learn unconsciously.

04 Pacing profile

“The short paragraph is always the pivot.”

Paragraph lengths, sentence cadence, and pivot placement are fingerprinted; repeated pacing shapes are blocked.

05 Question mix

“Q1 is main idea; the last one is tone.”

Question types, order, and difficulty curves are deliberately varied—there is no fixed question formula to learn.

06 Wrong-answer logic

“Eliminate the extreme option and the too-broad option—done.”

The way wrong options are wrong keeps changing. Elimination heuristics that work on one set fail on the next.

07 Voice separation

“All these passages read like the same writer.”

Passages use distinct authorial voices, verified as statistically different writers using authorship-forensics methods.

08 Answer-key hygiene

“When in doubt, pick the longest option. Or C.”

Letters, option lengths, and hedging of correct answers are tested for exploitable patterns across your whole series—not only within one set.

09 Topic distance

“We’ve basically seen this passage before.”

Content-level similarity is checked against every set in your history—the oldest check, still enforced.

The crackability check. Periodically we ask a strong analysis system: given the last ten passages in your series, can you predict the structure of the next one? If prediction improves, exposed patterns rotate out before students can hunt them. We simulate the pattern-hunting topper so yours finds nothing to hunt.

Topper shortcuts we remove by design

  • “Pick C when unsure.” Answer letters are planned across the set so keys cannot cluster into a learnable pattern.
  • “The longest option is right.” Length of the correct option is controlled within the set and watched across your series.
  • “The most hedged option is right.” Qualification style varies with voice; over-hedged options are sometimes deliberately wrong.
  • “Extreme words mean wrong.” Traps come from the passage’s logic—a misreading a strong reader could make—not from always/never flags.
