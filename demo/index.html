Most of what this engine writes never ships.

This is a production pipeline that produces CAT-style reading-comprehension sets under a weekly delivery contract. It does not write a passage and hand it over. It samples a structural blueprint, renders against it, then tries to kill its own output at seven separate gates — fingerprinting every candidate and scoring it against the whole corpus. What follows is the real audit trail.

The corpus

Quality is measured, not asserted.

Every set carries three independent numbers: a rubric score from a judge model, a compliance F1 measuring how faithfully the finished passage realised the structural brief it was given, and a novelty composite scoring it against its nearest neighbour in the corpus. None of them is self-reported by the model that wrote the passage.

Each set is assembled from seven independently sampled slots. Repetition in any one slot is tolerable; repetition of a combination is what the audit hunts for.

The rejection cascade

Seven gates, cheapest first.

Gates are ordered by cost. A topic collision is caught before a single word is written; a full nine-channel audit only runs on candidates that already survived everything upstream. That ordering is the whole economics of the pipeline — expensive rendering is never spent on a candidate that was always going to collide.

How it runs

A pipeline, not a prompt.

Generation is one stage out of eight. The rest is sampling, measurement and refusal — the parts that make output repeatable at a weekly cadence instead of good once.

01
Blueprint sampling

Seven structural slots — argument family, authorial persona, ending behaviour, sentence rhythm, revelation pattern, distractor profile, question topology — are drawn under constraints that penalise combinations the corpus has already used. The result is a specification, not a topic.

02
Pre-checks

Topic, movement skeleton and question topology are compared against the corpus before any rendering happens. A collision here costs nothing to discard.

03
Seeded composition

A retrieval layer supplies a real essay as conceptual seed material. The passage is composed to satisfy the blueprint's paragraph-function plan and word budget, not to summarise the seed — source attribution stays attached to every set.

04
Compliance scoring

The finished passage is parsed back into its realised structure and scored against the brief it was given. An F1 below threshold sends it back rather than forward.

05
Question engineering

Questions are built to the sampled topology, and every wrong option is assigned a named trap type — scope inflation, straw version, half truth, category slip, stance misread. A distractor with no mechanism is a defect, not a filler.

06
Independent solve

A separate model attempts the set without seeing the answer key, reporting per-question confidence and raising disputes. Disagreement between the key and the solver routes the set to review.

07
Multi-channel novelty audit

The finished set is fingerprinted — movement string, commitment curve, rhythm vector, topology signature, trap histogram, answer-letter sequence, stylometry, dense embedding, and the ordered rhetorical-move signature — and compared against every set in the trailing window. Nine channels carry a weighted similarity composite; novelty is one minus that score, taken against the closest neighbour rather than the average one.

08
Corpus hygiene

Beyond the individual set, the corpus itself is monitored: slot distribution drift, answer-letter runs, and a budget on how often the correct option may be the longest one — the single most exploitable tell in a coached test-taker's toolkit.
