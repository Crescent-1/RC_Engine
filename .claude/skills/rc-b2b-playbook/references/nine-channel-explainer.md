## THE NOVELTY AUDIT
### Nine ways we guarantee your mocks can't be pattern-matched

Serious aspirants don't just learn concepts — they learn *your content's habits*.
Where the passage turns. How the author hedges. Which option "sounds" correct.
Every set we deliver is statistically audited on nine independent channels against
the complete history of everything we've ever delivered to you. If any channel
says a pattern is forming, the set never ships.

| # | Channel | What students exploit | What we enforce |
|---|---------|----------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Argument skeleton** | "These passages always argue the same way." | Every passage is built on one of 32 distinct argumentative skeletons; no two recent sets share one, and your corpus stays balanced across all of them. |
| 2 | **Paragraph choreography** | "Para 2 always raises the objection, para 4 always resolves it." | The sequence of argumentative moves is tracked like a chess line — repeated move-orders are detected and rejected. |
| 3 | **Conviction arc** | "The author always starts neutral and commits late — so the late paragraphs hold the answers." | We measure how strongly the author appears committed to the thesis, paragraph by paragraph, and force that arc to vary set to set. This rhythm is the #1 thing students unconsciously learn. |
| 4 | **Pacing profile** | "The short paragraph is always the pivot." | Paragraph lengths, sentence cadence, and pivot placement are fingerprinted; repeated pacing shapes are blocked. |
| 5 | **Question mix** | "Q1 is always the main-idea question; the last one is always tone." | Question types, order, and difficulty curves are deliberately varied — there is no fixed question formula to learn. |
| 6 | **Wrong-answer logic** | "Eliminate the extreme option and the too-broad option — done." | The *way* wrong options are wrong keeps changing. Elimination heuristics that work on one set fail on the next — students are forced to actually read. |
| 7 | **Voice separation** | "All these passages read like the same writer." | Passages are written in 20 distinct authorial voices, verified as statistically different writers using the same stylometric methods used in authorship forensics. |
| 8 | **Answer-key hygiene** | "When in doubt, pick the longest / most hedged / option C." | Letters, option lengths, and hedging of correct answers are tested for exploitable patterns across your whole series — not just within one set. |
| 9 | **Topic distance** | "We've basically seen this passage before." | Content-level similarity is checked against every set in your history — the oldest trick, still enforced. |

### The crackability probe
Once a week we run the harshest test we know: we show an advanced analysis system
the last ten passages from your series and ask it to *predict the structure of the
next one* — where the thesis appears, where the reversal lands, how it will end,
what the questions will ask. If it starts guessing right, your students eventually
would too — and we rotate the exposed patterns out before that happens. **We
simulate the pattern-hunting topper so your real ones find nothing to hunt.**

### What we don't do
- No set ships from a generator alone. Every set passes an independent blind-solve
  check (a separate system must arrive at the key without seeing it), an editorial
  rubric, and human faculty review.
- Answer explanations name the exact misreading each wrong option was designed to
  catch — your faculty get teaching notes, not just keys.
- Your content is exclusively yours at the structural level: no other client of
  ours ever receives a set built on the same argumentative skeleton. This is
  enforced by our production database, and we can demonstrate it on request.

*Why this justifies a premium: a mock series that students can pattern-match
doesn't just get boring — it silently inflates their scores and breaks your
percentile predictions. Content that resists pattern-matching keeps your mock
data honest. That's what you're buying.*
