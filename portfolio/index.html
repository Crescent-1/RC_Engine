01 — Thesis

After a few hundred passages, students stop noticing topics. They notice structure.

Fixed prompts manufacture sameness—the same argumentative rhythm in a new costume. I treat structure as a sampled variable: combinatorial blueprints, exclusion windows, multi-model adjudication, and a nine-channel auditor that treats semantics as only one signal among many.

02 — Flagship

Structural-novelty
content engine

A commercial multi-stage pipeline that ships exam-grade CAT Reading Comprehension sets every week—under contract, under cost, under structural audit.

A

Blueprints, not prompts

A local composer samples from combinatorial libraries—32 argument families; 20 each of personas, endings, rhythms, revelations, distractor profiles, and question topologies—under exclusion windows, usage-decay weighting, and named compatibility rules.

B

Models by job

Opus renders passages. Sonnet refines blueprints and adversarially blind-solves questions. Haiku audits structural compliance and scores against blueprint-derived rubrics. Each call is pre-priced in a cost ledger—overspend is impossible by construction.

C

Nine channels of novelty

Paragraph-function Levenshtein, DTW on authorial commitment curves, Burrows’ Delta stylometry, Jensen–Shannon on distractor mechanisms, χ² answer-pattern tests, embedding cosine—and more. Similarity is multi-axis, not a single vector.

D

Gates before spend

Cheap local checks fire before expensive APIs. Failed structure yields targeted regeneration directives—not full re-runs. SQLite holds the full audit trail: blueprints, fingerprints, novelty scores, per-call spend, corpus-health drift.
