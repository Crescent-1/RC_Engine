[PASSAGE]

Sonia Marchetti's objection deserves to be stated at its full strength, and it runs roughly as follows: that the wave of wildcat walkouts moving through a continental auto-parts network between 2019 and 2023 was not a metaphorical epidemic but a literal one, differing from a respiratory virus only in the substrate of transmission. The claim was not made loosely. A stamping plant outside Saltillo goes down over a shift-bidding dispute. Within eleven weeks, seventeen downstream and lateral sites have stopped. The mapping is exact: susceptible workforces, infected workforces, recovered workforces that have burned through their appetite for risk and will not walk again for a year. Compartments, transitions, rates. What the borrowed apparatus offers is a number — R₀, the count of secondary walkouts a single walkout tends to generate under a given network configuration — and with it a way of asking where in a supply chain the transmission coefficient runs high, rather than the older and less tractable question of which workforces are militant. Grievance becomes pathogen. Contact becomes conduit. The break-room, the shared shuttle route, the union hall two towns over: these become edges in a graph, and the graph, not the temperament of any workforce, is what determines whether a local stoppage dies out or propagates. There is genuine explanatory purchase here. It should be conceded early, and completely.

And the fit is not approximate. Plotted against calendar weeks, strike incidence across forty-one sites traces the standard curve — the shallow rise, the steep middle, the long attenuating tail — with a residual small enough to embarrass most models in the labor literature. Patient zero is identifiable to the shift. R₀ comes out near 1.7 under most configurations, comfortably above the threshold at which propagation sustains itself. From this the transmission claim seems to follow directly: a curve of that shape, generated by that generative process, is a curve produced by contact. The inference feels closed. What the fit actually licenses, however, is narrower than what it appears to license, and the gap between the two is where the whole argument lives.

The validation was performed on the aggregate. The causal claim operates on the individual. Those are different levels, and a curve fitted at the first does not certify a mechanism posited at the second. Plant-level records, once disaggregated, show stoppages at nine sites with no traceable contact channel to Saltillo — no shared local, no overlapping rotation, no correspondence. What they share instead is a pay schedule frozen since 2017 and the same subcontracted safety regime. Independent accumulations reaching threshold on parallel clocks will, when smoothed weekly, draw the identical curve. Ignition, not infection.

The forecasting value survives this intact, and that is precisely the difficulty. No competing model predicts scale and tempo half as well, so the apparatus is not discardable; but its accuracy is purchased with a false account of why. Splitting the difference — calling it partly contagion, partly ignition — is unavailable, since the two make incompatible claims about the same nine plants. The curve can be kept. The cause cannot be kept alongside it, or not, at any rate, on the evidence currently in hand.
