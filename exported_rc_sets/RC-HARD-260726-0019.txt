[PASSAGE]

In the clerk's office at the back of a brick municipal building, a green steel cabinet held the deed transfers for a single decade; by 1968 the microfilm reader had been installed in the same room, and the cabinet was emptied over three afternoons. What followed was orderly. Each year a defensible fraction of paper originals was withdrawn from storage once filming had been verified, the verification itself conducted by a second reading of every frame, and each withdrawal was recorded on a form retained — inevitably — in paper. Storage costs fell. Retrieval times fell. When magnetic tape arrived, then relational tables, then an off-site provider whose racks nobody in the town has seen, the same logic extended without visible strain, since improvement in a system of this kind tends to be measured against the configuration it replaces rather than against the configuration it began from.

Granted: no single migration was reckless, and by the clerk's own accounting — page counts, field counts, frames verified — the same information crossed each interval intact. That accounting is not being disputed here. What is being proposed is that a records office can be locally faithful at every transfer and still, across the sequence, stop being an archive at all, having become an instrument of a different kind while every gauge on it read normal.

Consider what a deed in the green cabinet was. Ink pressed by a specific hand. A crease where it had been folded to fit a pocket. Coffee. The document was a trace: caused by the event, physically continuous with it. A scan is not caused by the event. It is caused by the document, and shaped by what the retrieval system needs — indexed fields, normalized dates, a resolution chosen to make text legible and everything else negligible. So the object migrated from being evidence to being an answer. To consult the town record now is to query, and a query returns what was made findable. Nothing else is present to be overlooked.

Where, then? The first microfilming preserved every mark on the page, so not there. The first database entry only added an index; the images stayed. The first search-replacing-a-folder-pull changed no bits. And bits do match: checksums run monthly, migrations validated, corruption caught. Every candidate threshold, examined closely, turns out to have been the one that changed nothing.

Which is the finding, not the failure to reach one. The kind-shift occurred, is irreversible, and is not located in any transfer, because it is a property of transferring rather than of transfers — each of which is authenticated by whichever custodian holds the current format, using criteria supplied by that format, so that fidelity can be certified indefinitely while the standard of fidelity migrates too. Nobody in the chain was in a position to see it; the position does not exist within the chain. This is a real conclusion and it costs something to hold. It forbids the remedy it implies: there is no migration to have refused, no year to have stopped at, no cabinet whose emptying can be identified as the wrong one, and so the town's memory of itself remains fully documented, monthly verified, and no longer of a kind that can be checked against anything but itself.
