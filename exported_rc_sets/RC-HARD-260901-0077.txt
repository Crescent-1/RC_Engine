[PASSAGE]

"Debriefing" here means a procedure that assumes the informant knows more than she has said, and that a structured sequence of prompts will draw it out intact. That assumption is what the sighting report form encodes. Consider a single filed sheet from a civilian reporting hotline. Two pages. The first page is almost entirely closed fields: date, local time to the minute, latitude and longitude or nearest crossroads, weather, moon phase, whether the observer wore corrective lenses. Then the shape checklist — disc, cigar, triangle, sphere, chevron, "other (specify)" — and beside it a duration field ruled for seconds and minutes, as though the witness had been holding a stopwatch. Angular size is to be given by the coin method: the width of a dime at arm's length, a quarter, a thumbnail. Then the confidence scale, one to five. The narrative box, the only place where the witness may say what she saw in her own words, is three ruled lines long. It sits below the fold. Everything above it has already taught her what counts as a fact. The ritual requires that a private, unrepeatable, badly lit fragment of memory be sorted into the hotline's vocabulary before a single reader touches it, and the sorting is done by the witness herself, alone at a kitchen table, hours or years after the fact. She is both informant and first analyst. She decides that the thing was a chevron because chevron is on the list. She rounds 9:40-ish to 21:37 because the field has a slot for minutes and no slot for "ish." Informants report that this feels like being helped. It is help of a kind. The finished sheet has the density of an instrument reading: coordinates, arcminutes, a numbered confidence. Nothing on it lies. Every number was supplied in good faith by someone who was there, and the precision belongs to the paper.

Nearly every sighting form in circulation looks like this one. Not by coincidence. They descend from aviation incident reports, which were designed for crews with instruments, logs, and a filing deadline measured in hours. The lineage shows in the field order. What the borrowing never carried across is the thing that made those fields sensible: a cockpit, a clock, a duty to write it down before landing.

Widen the frame and the same design logic turns up wherever a lay witness is asked to file an experience into categories built for aggregation — the traffic accident diagram with its little numbered cars, the intake symptom checklist with its ten-point pain face, the campus hate-incident form with its dropdown of protected characteristics — instruments that differ enormously in how soon and how directly they are filled out, and that therefore distort in very different amounts, but that share one habit: they retain the testimony and discard the conditions under which it was given.

What such forms produce is not a record of uncertain events but a uniform-looking evidentiary object manufactured out of radically non-uniform acts of remembering. The distortion is real and it runs one way, toward false consistency. Analysts owe the archive an account of how each sheet was made. Designers owe it a field for the witness's own hedge. And the aggregate owes its readers a warning that it is smoother than anything anyone ever saw.
