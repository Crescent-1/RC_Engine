## 1. The three length tells — broken

The headline defect. Before and after:

| Paper | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| RC-ELITE-260711-0025 | correct = **longest in 6 of 6** | longest in **1 of 8**, shortest in 0 |
| RC-HARD-260726-0021 | correct = **shortest in 6 of 6** | longest in **0 of 8**, shortest in 0 |
| RC-HARD-260726-0025 | correct = **shortest in 6 of 6** | longest in 2 of 8, shortest in 1 |

The cause was a consistent drafting habit, not chance. In 0711-0025 the correct answers carried the full reasoning while distractors were single clauses; in 0021 and 0025 the reverse — correct answers were terse and true, distractors padded with explanatory subordinate clauses. Fixing it meant redistributing that length rather than trimming: correct answers gained the qualifying phrase they had been denied, and distractors gave up padding that added nothing.

0726-0025 Q1 went from **1.79×** (94 vs 168 characters — the widest in the corpus) to 1.17×.

Four papers carried only six questions. Two of those six were assumption items and had to go, so **ten questions were written**, each with four balanced options and a full four-tag elimination key.

| Paper | Added | Type | Replaced |

| RC-ELITE-260711-0025 | Q7, Q8 | analogy; weaken | — |

Types were chosen from what each paper lacked. 0726-0032 had no central-idea question at all, which is why one was added there rather than another application item. **The two removed stems**, both a new variant worth adding to your automated check:

> "…depends on which **unstated assumption**?"

Previous instances used "would best license the inference". Match on both.
