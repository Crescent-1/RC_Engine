In March 2021 a county planner drove out to Palm Bend Kennel Club with a clipboard and a checklist of eleven items, and could not tick the first one, which read: *closure complete (Y/N)*. The referendum that ended commercial greyhound racing in the district had passed by nine points; the ordinance implementing it made the word do heavy lifting. Certified complete, the site's final tax abatements release. The operator's performance bond returns. The parcel becomes reclassifiable, which is to say sellable to whoever wants forty-one acres near an interstate exit. So the word is not decorative. It is a switch. Somebody has to define it precisely enough to sign a name under, and the ordinance, in the manner of ordinances, simply used it.

Take the thinnest candidate: closure means the legal cessation of racing. Satisfied at 10:42 p.m. on 27 March 2021, when the last box opened. But the county's own file kept adding entries for months afterward, all of them concerning grooms, kennel hands, and dogs — which suggests the thing being tracked was never the racing. Racing stopped in an evening. What the ordinance was pricing was consequence.

Try instead the physical candidate: closure means decommissioning — track surface, kennel compounds, tote board, grandstand. This one at least has a shape you can walk around. It also went badly. Demolition contracts sat unsigned for eleven months while an asbestos survey worked through the 1962 clubhouse; the kennel compounds stayed up, roofs intact, colonised by feral cats, because no line item in the award covered removing them; the starting-gate mechanism was cut up for scrap in month fourteen by a firm that paid by weight. The engineer who filed the survey noted, in a memorandum the county later attached to the file, that his scope covered structures and not obligations. Which is the trouble. Under this candidate the county could certify completion the morning the bulldozers arrived, with 340 grooms and handlers still unpaid, unnotified, and reading about the timeline in a trade newsletter.

Push the other way, then. Closure means the last displaced worker is back in work. Humane, and useless. Three kennel hands were filing claims twenty-six months on. The state transition fund's own eligibility clause runs until need ceases, with no terminal date. A definition that cannot ever be satisfied is not a stricter version of the county's question. It is a different question, wearing the county's clothes — and the parties arguing about when Palm Bend closed were never disagreeing about dates. They were disagreeing about who counts as a party to the closing.

So the survivor: closure means every party's contractual claim against the operator is settled — dog placements executed, severance paid where owed, vendors discharged. Not that this makes the closing just; it makes it *answerable*. A claim is settled or it isn't. Eighteen months, against the six the ordinance imagined, and the eighteen only arrived because 214 of the 340 worker claims were written off as unrecoverable against a shell with no assets. Written off is a settlement. That is the price of the definition, and it is a real price. The planner signed the first line of the checklist in September 2022, having satisfied himself that nothing further could be collected, which is not the same as having collected it.
