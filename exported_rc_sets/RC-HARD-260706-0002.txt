[PASSAGE]

The question was answered a long time ago, and answered well. Free will is a confusion. This is not the shrill verdict of a partisan; it is the calm settlement of a field that has done its due diligence, weighed the alternatives with unusual patience, and found only one survivor. The libertarian's uncaused cause was inspected and found to be a superstition wearing a lab coat. The compatibilist's rescue—will is freedom from constraint, not from causation—was tested against ordinary usage and held. Determinism absorbed the rest. Even the holdouts concede the machinery: neurons fire on schedule, and the schedule does not consult us. Every serious survey confirms the drift. The textbooks agree. The philosophers of mind agree, more or less, in the way people agree who no longer expect to be surprised. What remains is bookkeeping. One tidies the vocabulary, retires the theological residue, and moves on to problems with a pulse. The matter is closed. The clock, so to speak, has been opened, and the movement inside runs exactly as predicted.

How did it close? Not by a single proof but by a slow attrition of opponents. Each generation inherited the previous generation's exhaustion. The libertarian position grew tiresome to defend, so fewer defended it; fewer defenders meant weaker defense, which confirmed the weakness. There is a bolder reading available, and it has adherents: that the debate was never about metaphysics at all, only about which words we are permitted to keep. On this view the whole quarrel dissolves into grammar, and the winner is simply whoever last controlled the dictionary. Tidy. It has the appeal of every position that mistakes its own confidence for depth.

Notice what the attrition selected for. Not truth—stamina. A verdict reached by the exhaustion of dissent records who tired first, not who was right. The consensus is a fact about the debaters, not about the will. This is the delayed referent: the "due diligence" of the opening was never diligence about free will; it was diligence about maintaining agreement. A field can be scrupulous about its manners and careless about its object. The machinery ran as predicted because it was built to. That is watchmaking, not discovery.

So the tempting middle is refused. One could say the question is answered *and* the process was flawed, and split the difference. No. A conclusion certified by fatigue is not half-earned; it is uncertified. Whether free will survives inspection remains genuinely open, precisely because the inspection never happened. The clock keeps time. Nobody checked it against the sun.
