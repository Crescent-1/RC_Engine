[PASSAGE]

Orwell despised the tea-stained bureaucratic memo. He wanted prose you could see through, like a clean window onto its object. His heirs turned that preference into a political claim: unadorned sentences are a civic instrument through which citizens govern themselves. Ornate writing, on this account, hoards meaning behind a screen only initiates can part. Plain writing, on this account, returns meaning to the reader. No priest, no glossator, no seminar stands between the sentence and the reader who must weigh it. A pamphlet that any farmer can parse is more democratic than a treatise that needs a professor. Say what you mean. The argument has an honest appeal, and its champions have never lacked for examples.

The complication begins with the person doing the praising. To call a sentence "plain" rather than merely flat, to distinguish earned clarity from thin naivety, to hear the difference between Hemingway's restraint and the blankness of a manual — this is connoisseurship. Where the classical rhetorician graded ornament, the modern critic grades its absence, and grading is grading whichever pole you stand at. The eye that certifies plainness has been trained to certify. So the very judgment meant to dissolve expertise turns out to be one of its finer exercises: the reader is told he needs no specialist, by a specialist ruling on what needs no specialist.

A defense of the critic remains available. Perhaps his skill serves rather than supplants the reader's — a translator's expertise, which exists to be spent and disappear, not a customs officer's, which exists to detain. The critic clears the road; the farmer still does the driving. It is a graceful defense, and I would like it to hold. Its weakness appears when the defense must become specific. Which sentences count as plain? Whose reading counts as competent enough to confirm that they are? Every answer smuggles the officer back into the translator's coat. To assemble the demonstrating examples, someone must already know which prose democratizes and which merely flatters the untrained; to certify the judging reader, someone must set the bar. The service, described, becomes the substitution again. And here the essay's real quarry steps into view. The question was never which prose serves self-government. It is whether any critical vocabulary can name that service without the naming itself performing the authority it disowns — whether the border can be described from a position not already inside one country.

Which leaves this essay's author where the argument put everyone else. I have ranked Orwell above the manual, called one clarity earned and another thin, decided which cases would carry the point — the discriminating work, done in plain view, in the act of warning against it. Refusing to judge would not release me. It would only be a judgment of a quieter kind, relocating the critic one room over in the same building. The debate closes with each side still in arrears. The democrat owes an account of taste that does not need a taster. The skeptic owes a reason his exposure is not itself another connoisseur's verdict, dressed down. Neither debt, so far as these sentences can reach, has been paid.
