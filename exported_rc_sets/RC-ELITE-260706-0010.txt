[PASSAGE]

The story as it is usually handed down runs in one direction, unbroken: as villages thickened into cities and cities into kingdoms, the tangle of obligation outgrew the memory of any elder or the cadence of any recited oath, until the need to hold a rule steady across seasons and successors called forth the marked surface, and law, once sung, was set down to be seen.

The clay refuses this tidiness. Long before any tablet names a penalty or fixes a right, the earliest marks count things. Sheep. Jars of oil. Owed measures of grain. The proto-cuneiform of Uruk, the tokens sealed in Mesopotamian bullae, the notched bones some prehistorians read as reckonings — these record what is owed, not what is forbidden. Assyriologists date the accounting centuries ahead of anything a jurist would recognize. So the arrow, if it points anywhere, points the other way: marking came first, and rules arrived to inhabit a technology already built for debt.

Yet where the Mesopotamian record invites that reversal, the transactional evidence itself suggests something less linear. To mark a debt is already to assert that a claim persists, that it can be checked, that a third party might be summoned to confirm it — and a checkable, persisting, enforceable claim is not far from the germ of a rule. Chinese oracle-bone scholars note the same braiding: the notation that fixes an obligation is also the occasion of the dispute that demands sharper notation. Each transaction did both at once. The scratch that recorded the loan created the very possibility of contesting it, and the contest pressed the scratch toward precision. One cannot easily say the marking preceded the rule when the rule was what made the marking worth keeping.

Push the loop one turn further and the ground gives way entirely. A token that records a binding claim presupposes a community that already treats certain claims as binding — that already distinguishes the owed from the given, the borrowed from the seized. But such a distinction cannot be held in common, negotiated, disputed, and honored without something already language-like doing the negotiating; the Egyptologist and the Vedic philologist arrive at the same wall from opposite roads. So the token does not stand at an origin. Behind it lies a settled agreement about obligation; behind that agreement lies the communicative work that made agreement possible; and that work, once it grows durable, is indistinguishable from the marking we set out to explain. The comfortable position was always the sequential one — that we could name a first term and derive the second. The burden now sits with whoever would defend an order at all: let them produce the token that binds without a prior rule, or the rule that binds without a prior sign, and hold either up unaccompanied. Neither has yet been found, and the loop admits us at any point while granting no entrance that is also a beginning.
