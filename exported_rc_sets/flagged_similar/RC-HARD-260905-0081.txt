[PASSAGE]

An instrument records what it was built to record, and its silences are rarely accidental. When a planning department in a mid-sized city commissioned an audit of its derelict properties — the trigger was a partial wall collapse at the old gasworks, and the department's counsel wanted a defensible order of operations — the brief specified three scored columns: structural hazard, soil contamination, demolition cost. Twelve sites were surveyed over four months. The audit behaved like an insurance adjuster walking a fire-damaged house: it priced the joists, tested the flue, noted the cracked lintel, and reported faithfully on everything an adjuster is trained to see. An adjuster does not ask whether the family still eats in the kitchen. That is not negligence. It is the shape of the form. And a form, once approved, tends to be mistaken for the world it was drawn from.

The numbers were real. Gasworks: hazard 8.4, sealing cost $310,000. The old rail cooperage: 6.1, $88,000. Engineers called the instrument sound. A neighbourhood association chair told the council the audit "measured the buildings and called that the site" — a sentence worth keeping, though not quite as she meant it.

She meant the audit had asked too narrow a question. The record supports something earlier than that. The design phase was not where the failure occurred; the columns were competently specified for the assumption they served, which was that a derelict property is a property in zero current use. Derelict described the roofs. It was silently extended to the people. And there the parallel breaks: a collapsing wall and an unoccupied lot are not two readings of the same emptiness, since one is a fact about masonry and the other a claim about human beings that nobody had gone to check. Scores of them used the cooperage yard weekly. The audit had no column that could have held them, because the officials who wrote the brief had already settled what use meant before an engineer set foot on gravel.

The department proposed a comment period. Notice, thirty days, a hearing. Comment periods reward the organised. The families who bring folding chairs on Sunday mornings do not file letters, do not attend, and leave nothing a clerk could later cite — which is precisely the form of use the audit had erased.

That the hearing fix fails is not evidence that informal use resists counting. It counts easily; it simply requires being present when it happens. A graduate researcher, working alone with a clipboard, visited eight of the twelve sites at hours the engineers had no reason to keep — half past seven on Saturdays, dusk on weekdays — and logged what she found: a table assembled from pallet wood, a clearing swept with a shop broom, a hopscotch grid chalked on the cooperage slab and re-chalked after rain. Grant the engineers everything. Their hazard figures were accurate and their cost estimates held. The temptation is to say both instruments were partly right and should be averaged, and that verdict should be refused, because an average of a correct measurement and an absent one is simply the correct measurement with the absence hidden inside it. What closes the gap is the visit. Eleven people at the cooperage, 7:40 a.m., one thermos.
