[PASSAGE]

Any count of a moving population is a bargain between what matters and what can be seen. I fixed a definition first and worked outward: a person is homeless when there is no stable, private place to which they can reliably return. A man in a shop doorway fails that test in every clause. Take a family rotating between two aunts and a friend on a six-week loop. Every night they have a roof, a door, a floor they are welcome on. Nothing they can return to. By my definition they are homeless. By the street count they do not exist. By the statutory returns they exist only if they walk into a council office and are found to be owed a duty. England runs both. The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government publishes them apart: the single-night rough sleeping snapshot in one release, the statutory homelessness statistics, households owed a prevention or relief duty, in the other, and its own notes warn that the two do not measure the same population. The second number is many times the first. It counts households rather than people, and by legal test rather than by sighting.

On counting night in London the outreach teams walk routes they know by heart and find fewer people than the week before, some taken into a night shelter, some riding a bus, some simply moved on, while the council records fill with households in hotel rooms, hostels and flats too small for them, none visible from any pavement. Others appear in neither, having learned to avoid both.

Beds follow the number. So do outreach shifts and the grant money a council can argue for. Meanwhile rents climb, nightly lets churn, and people cross between the two categories faster than either system records them. I came to this expecting an argument about arithmetic. It is an argument about which kind of instability the state is built to notice, and whose therefore gets a bed.

Side by side they look like two imperfect estimates of one thing. They are not the same kind of object. The snapshot records an absence of people from places on one night: checkable, local, stale within the week. The statutory figure records a relationship: a household asked, a council assessed, a duty attached. That catches insecurity with no visible form, the aunt's back room, the notice expiring on Friday. It catches only those who ask, and asking takes a working phone and a free afternoon. Neither number is the condition I set out. Both miss it, and each looks foolish beside the other. They fail in opposite directions, and the failures do not cost the same. Which would I print as the national figure? The records-based one. Its errors are the sort more staff and better intake can shrink; the snapshot's error is built in, since it only counts what stands in view. Keep the snapshot regardless; it is the only instrument that answers tonight. And keep it ready: when the case-level records behind the second figure change shape again, and the contracts on the hotels and nightly lets that absorbed the last surge run out, the gap between the two will move for reasons unconnected to how many people lost a place they could return to.
