[PASSAGE]

A service can be dismantled without anyone deciding to dismantle it. Marlowe's 2022 move of school-meal assistance onto an online renewal portal was read, at the time, as a decision with intent behind it. Renewal deadlines passed. Children queued with no credit on their accounts. The commentary arrived quickly. The portal was described as an austerity device wearing a user interface, or a surveillance apparatus harvesting household detail, or a vendor's exclusion machine sold to a city that wanted fewer claimants and knew how to get them. Each account had the virtue of naming somebody. Each assumed that a system which sheds families must have been built to shed them.

The records, read in sequence, tell a duller story. A household files a renewal; the address on the form is the new flat, the address on the tax record is the old one; the match fails and the case is routed to manual review. A payslip is photographed at an angle, uploaded, and placed in a verification queue behind several hundred others. A warning notice is generated and sent to the email account the household abandoned two years earlier when the previous caseworker set it up. Nothing here is a glitch, though it is tempting to file it as one — the point is not that each step malfunctioned but that each step worked exactly as specified while passing the case to the next queue. The office holding that queue is staffed for a volume it stopped receiving in 2019. Cases accumulate at the narrowest section of the pipe. By the time a human opens the file, the school term has started.

What the portal did was not digitise a service. It rearranged the order of operations. Verification that once happened after enrolment now happens before it. Uncertainty that a clerk once absorbed is now held by the household, and the household cannot resolve it, because resolution sits in another office's queue. So the delay is not the sum of the errors. It is the shape of the sequence. Every small ambiguity waits on a body of work no one has been given time to do, and waiting is what the design turned into a rule.

Against this it can be objected that one district in Marlowe kept its meal enrolments almost flat through the same period, on the same portal, with the same vendor — which looks like proof that the software was never the problem. It is not. That district retained two clerks with standing authority to accept a mismatched address on the strength of a school register, and it kept a paper route open through the front office. The portal there was one channel among several, not the only aperture. Reproducing that across the city would mean staffed help desks, discretion written into the caseworker's authority rather than into the matching rule, paper preserved as an equal path, and success measured by enrolments completed rather than by transactions processed per hour. All of that is available and none of it is cheap. A better system would not remove the work; it would only relocate the place where a person has to sit and absorb the doubt, and the city adopted this one precisely because it appeared to have abolished that person.
