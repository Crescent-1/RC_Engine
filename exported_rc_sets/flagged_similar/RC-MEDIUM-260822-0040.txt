[PASSAGE]

What, in Francis Bacon’s New Atlantis, is being measured when Salomon’s House measures its achievements? Imagine two surveyors standing before the same excavated wall. One counts every exposed stone, noting its size, source and relation to its neighbours; the other paces the length of wall still standing and asks whether it can bear a roof. The House offers both procedures. Its “merchants of light” travel abroad for books and experiments; its “depredators” gather material from authors; its “compilers” arrange the findings. In a side room one can almost see the narrow labels tied to jars and instruments, each carrying a place, a trial, a result. Knowledge, on this account, is what has been recovered and made comparable. Yet other officers are assigned to draw new experiments from old ones, to seek “light” from practice, and to turn discoveries toward works. Here the unit is not the report but the alteration: a changed fruit, a quieter disease, a manufactured effect.

The archive school and the workshop school have therefore read the same institution in opposite ways. For the former, its vast intake is the achievement; for the latter, intake is merely raw ore. Not a contrast between passive record and active invention, rather a contrast between two forms of disciplined attention, each requiring the other’s materials. Still, the workshop reading has an obvious force: an untested report can remain only a well-filed rumour.

By the 1970s, “information” had already come to suggest transferable content, as though a fact retained its force while passing from hand to hand. Bacon’s vocabulary is less settled. “Light” names both finding and making. “Works” names outcomes. “Experiments” names occasions arranged to yield further occasions. The House’s sequence is plain: collect reports, sort them, alter conditions, compare effects, then return the result to the store of reports.

Its celebrated motto, “the knowledge of Causes, and secret motions of things; and the enlarging of the bounds of Human Empire,” is often made to settle the matter in favour of mastery. It does not. The phrase joins an account of causes to an enlargement of power, but it never supplies a common scale on which a classified observation and a successful operation can be weighed. The more consequential division lies instead in who may decide that one has become the other, since in that decision the House converts its crowded rooms of evidence into the authority claimed by its works.
