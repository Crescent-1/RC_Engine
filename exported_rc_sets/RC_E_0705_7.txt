[PASSAGE]

Wealth, we are told, corrodes the soul. The moralist's version of this claim is old and durable: proximity to great fortune breeds a species of exceptionalism, a quiet conviction that the ordinary frictions of life — waiting, scarcity, refusal — are contingencies that apply to other people. On this reading, the heir's problem is characterological. He becomes a certain kind of person because of what surrounds him, and the remedy, correspondingly, is characterological too: humility, downward mobility, the deliberate shedding of insulation. It is a satisfying story because it locates the disease in individuals and therefore promises a cure.

But the story misdescribes the mechanism.

What insulates the wealthy is not primarily a defect of feeling; it is a structure of exemption from consequence. The heir does not merely *feel* invincible — he has, in the most literal sense, been made exempt from the feedback that ordinarily disciplines belief. A poor decision costs him nothing that registers. Reality, for most people, is the name we give to the resistance that pushes back when we err; the very rich have purchased distance from that resistance, and distance from resistance is indistinguishable, phenomenologically, from being right. This is why the moralist's cure so often fails. You can exhort a man toward humility, but humility is a disposition that is *taught* by consequence, and you have removed the teacher. Voluntary descent, undertaken as a spiritual exercise, reproduces the very exemption it claims to renounce: the descent is elective, reversible, curated. One remains the author of one's own exposure, which is precisely not what exposure means.

So the structural account seems to defeat the moral one. It doesn't, quite.

Because the structural account, pressed to its conclusion, proves too much. If belief is disciplined only by consequence, and the rich are exempt from consequence, then their distorted self-understanding is not a failing they could be blamed for — it is simply what any of us would come to believe under identical conditions. The mechanism that explains the distortion also excuses it. And this is intolerable to the very intuition that set the inquiry in motion, because that intuition was not merely diagnostic; it was accusatory. We did not want to know only *why* the heir believes he deserves his position. We wanted to hold him responsible for continuing to believe it. The structural story, by dissolving the agent into his conditions, quietly evacuates the blame it was recruited to explain.

Perhaps responsibility survives at a different location. Not in the origin of the belief, which is indeed environmental, but in the refusal to interrogate it once its origin is visible. A man cannot help the conditions that formed his convictions; he can, arguably, notice that they were formed. Yet even this concedes more than it should. The capacity to notice — the reflective distance, the leisure for self-examination, the very language of critique — is itself unevenly distributed, itself a possession, arriving with the same insulation that made the noticing necessary. The instrument of the cure is manufactured by the disease.

We are left, then, with two accounts that cannot both be fully right and cannot be cleanly separated. The moral story needs an agent the structural story keeps dissolving; the structural story needs an exemption the moral story keeps trying to breach. Neither the estate nor the confession is quite the place where the reckoning could occur. It is not obvious that such a place exists.

**Hidden Structural Shifts**
The passage stages three reversals — moral account undercut by structural, structural undercut by its own exculpatory logic, relocated-responsibility undercut by the uneven distribution of reflection. Readers freeze at the structural critique (para 3), mistaking a way-station for the destination. The final paragraph withholds synthesis, which readers instinctively supply.

**Core Distractor Traps**
- Q1 (A): structural freezing — locks onto para 3 before the self-defeat in para 4.
- Q1 (D): false synthesis — offers the reconciliation the passage explicitly denies.
- Q3 (D): scope inversion — converts a lament about who *has* the instrument into a claim about who alone can *make* it.
- Q4 (D): local/global confusion — imports para 5's responsibility move where a consequence mechanism is asked.
- Q6 (C): framework importation — invents an unstated "third framework" the passage never gestures toward.

**Tonal Ambiguity**
The author advances each corrective with apparent conviction, then withdraws it, so no position is ever simply held. The tone is analytically committed to the movement itself rather than to any station within it. Its final posture is suspended dissatisfaction, not balanced neutrality.
