[PASSAGE]

Classification precedes judgment in most archives, and film festivals are archives that happen to run projectors. The intake desk logs each submission, records a runtime to the second, and assigns a category folder before any programmer has watched a frame. The folders are fixed; the reasoning is documentary rather than critical. When *Chaconne* arrives — eighty-one minutes, one composer, one violin part — the clerk performs the first step, which is to read the synopsis against the genre list and see which word it answers to.

Granted, the film does dramatise the composer's life: there are scenes with dialogue, a lead actor, sets built to a period specification, and a plot of sorts running from commission to first performance. That much is not in dispute, and the clerk records it. But the tagging rule is arithmetical rather than interpretive — a biopic must allocate a majority of runtime to dramatised narrative — and here the unbroken forty-three-minute performance sequence, filmed in a single take, never cutting away to the composer's story, occupies more than half of what was logged. So the concession is precisely what defeats the tag. The film is biographical in every respect except the one the folder measures, and the folder, having measured, returns the file automatically.

The clerk tries concert film next. Here the music-clearance office keeps its own checklist, and its first line item states that a concert film shall contain no scripted dramatic scenes with actors playing named historical figures. The workshop scenes disqualify it immediately. What we know at this point is only that one threshold was not met; how we know it is that a reviewer initialled a box, having watched, so far as the file records, nothing. Other thresholds were in principle reachable. This one was not.

Documentary remains. The verification desk requires — and this is a procedural safeguard, not an aesthetic preference, so it might be read as a small technical matter — that a documentary carry sourced captions or narration distinguishing attested fact from staged reconstruction. *Chaconne* withholds that distinction by design, letting invented dialogue sit uncaptioned beside a performance that actually occurred, so the desk, unable to certify which is which, returns the file unstamped.

Three folders, three rejections, each internally correct. What is left is the residual category — miscellaneous, jury discretion — which has no dedicated jury, no comparative pool, and, in the years for which the office keeps figures, the lowest programming rate of any folder it maintains. We should not say that the instruments were wrong; each measured accurately what it was calibrated to measure. Rather, the three of them together cannot register a film whose achievement consists in refusing to resolve into any one of them, and the intake system converts that refusal into a penalty at the point of logging, before evaluation begins. To conclude here would be to conclude that the film failed, which is the reading the procedure produces and the evidence does not support. Whether an archive can hold a work whose value lies in what its folders are built to exclude is not answered by the file, though the file, unstamped and shelved under discretion, is at least where the question would have to be asked.
